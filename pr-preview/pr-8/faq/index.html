Mission, Purpose, & Leadership

Why does the Rust Foundation exist?

The Rust Foundation was created in 2021 to serve as the nonprofit steward of the Rust programming language. We provide support, infrastructure, and financial and administrative aid to the Rust Project and support the decentralized Rust language community in various ways.

Our mission is for global computer systems to be more secure, sustainable, scalable, and efficient through the use of the Rust programming language.

You can learn more about the areas of focus we prioritize by visiting our About page.

What type of organization is the Rust Foundation?

The Rust Foundation is an independent nonprofit organization.

In legal terms, we are a non-stock corporation, organized under Delaware law, and claiming tax exemption from U.S. federal taxation under Section 501(c)(6) of the Internal Revenue Code. You can learn more about the 501(c)(6) classification from the Internal Revenue Service.

Who leads the Rust Foundation?

We are governed by the Rust Foundation Board of Directors, which is composed of Member Directors (voting representatives for our corporate members) and Project Directors (voting representatives championing the interests of the Rust Project). Our board of directors is responsible for voting on all major decisions about the Rust Foundation.

The Rust Foundation and its staff team are led by Executive Director & CEO, Dr. Rebecca Rumbul.

Learn more about our board and staff here.

What do Rust Foundation Project Directors do?

Rust Foundation Project Directors are elected representatives of the Rust Project and community on our board of directors, serving two-year terms.
Project Directors attend all board meetings where they vote on Foundation measures, raise Project topics for discussion, and lend a critical Rust Project/community perspective to all board discussions.

More information about the Project Director role can be found on our Community page.

How exactly does the Rust Foundation support the Rust community?

All Rust Foundation initiatives are designed to help support the growth and health of the Rust community.

Currently, the Rust Foundation provides direct support for global Rust community members through our Community Grants Program. We offer Fellowships for established Rust contributors, Project Grants to help fund discrete packages of Rust-related work, Event Support Grants to support Rust community organizers and their events, and Hardship Grants to Rust contributors facing urgent financial difficulties.

Definitions

How does the Rust Foundation define “Rust Project Leadership”?

In our communications, you might find references to “Rust Project leadership”. This term generally refers to the Leadership Council and the Rust Foundation’s Project Directors.

How does the Rust Foundation define the “Rust community”?

In our communications, you might find references to the “Rust community” or ecosystem. This is the term we use to encompass the general combination of Rust Project maintainers, contributors, students, educators, enthusiasts, and advocates at the organizational and individual levels.

How does the Rust Foundation define “stewardship” of the Rust language?

“Stewardship” generally refers to the job or responsibility of supervising, maintaining, and caring for something. 

In our case, the Rust Foundation serves as an independent nonprofit “steward” to help grow, support, and care for the Rust programming language. We do this by advocating for the Rust language in a variety of ways, providing legal and administrative support to community members and maintainers, and doing all we can to ensure the health and growth of the Rust programming language.

Finance, Funding, & Membership

How is the Rust Foundation funded?

The Rust Foundation is primarily funded through our membership dues as well as one-time contributions from both members and non-member organizations. 

Our membership investment information list listed in this deck. If you would like to support the Rust Foundation’s mission as a member, click here. 

If your organization would like to make a one-time donation, please contact us through this form and select “donations and in-kind support” from the menu of categories. Individuals can support our Community Grants Program by donating to the program through our GitHub Sponsorship page.

How does the Rust Foundation allocate its funding/resources?

The funding we receive through membership dues helps power a variety of needs across the Rust Foundation including operational expenses (administrative and legal fees, employee payroll, necessary staff travel, marketing, etc.), Rust language infrastructure support, community grants, and much more. Our board of directors reviews and approves our budget each year.

Organizational donors also have the option of earmarking donations for specific initiatives, which are discussed and approved by our board of directors. 

In 2024, we’ve allocated a specific amount of our funding to support priorities identified by leaders of the Rust Project. 

Recent information about our expenses and funding allocation can be found in our annual report.

I’m an individual interested in supporting the Rust Foundation. How can I get involved?

Individuals not representing a particular organization can support the Community Grants Program by donating to the program through our GitHub Sponsorship page.

What are the primary benefits of Rust Foundation membership?

Our members benefit from their involvement in the Rust Foundation in a variety of ways. These benefits include:

  • Thought leadership & networking opportunities
  • Business strategy & visibility
  • Rust community engagement & impact
  • Open source goodwill

… and more! Learn more about the benefits of Rust Foundation membership in our Membership Overview Deck.

How can my organization learn more about/pursue Rust Foundation membership?

Please visit our Membership & Donations hub for more information and to get involved in the Rust Foundation.

Can my organization support the Rust Foundation without being a member?

Absolutely! The Rust Foundation welcomes contributions from members and non-members alike. Please start by visiting our Membership & Donations hub and if you would like to discuss options, please visit our contact page to find a contact form and select the “donations & in-kind support” option prior to submission.  

Legal & Administrative Information

I’m a maintainer of/contributor to the Rust Project. What kind of legal support from the Rust Foundation is available to me?
How does the Rust Foundation define “support” and “stewardship” when it comes to the Rust Project and ecosystem?

The Rust Foundation supports a variety of administrative needs within the Rust ecosystem including…

  • Financing key tools and software behind the Rust language
  • Providing editorial support for the developing Rust language specification
  • Managing the technical infrastructure underlying the development of Rust
  • Managing and stewarding the Rust trademark and other assets of the Rust Foundation.
  • Managing Rust-focused events

Official definitions of administrative support provided to the Rust ecosystem can be found in section 1.3 of our bylaws.

Why does the Rust Foundation own the Rust trademarks?

Mozilla, the original home of the Rust project, transferred all trademark and infrastructure assets, including the crates.io package registry, to the Rust Foundation upon its formation in 2021. As such, stewarding and managing the Rust trademarks has been a responsibility codified in the Rust Foundation’s bylaws since our formation. 

As the owner of the Rust trademarks, the Rust Foundation manages the associated legal and administrative costs — a large responsibility that would otherwise fall on already overburdened community individuals.

Decision-Making & Collaboration

How can I propose a new Rust Foundation initiative/area of focus for consideration?

If you are a Rust community member and/or a Rust Project maintainer, we encourage you to reach out to the Rust Project Leadership Council with any suggestions for new Rust Foundation areas of focus. You are also welcome to email our team directly at contact@rustfoundation.org, however communicating with the elected Rust Project leadership council is the best way to surface and discuss your ideas initially. Learn more about the Rust Project Leadership Council and find contact information here.

If you represent a Rust Foundation member organization, please contact your representative on our board of directors to surface any ideas for new initiatives and areas of focus.

If you represent a non-member organization with a suggestion, please reach out to us through our contact form.

How exactly does the Rust Foundation assist and collaborate with the Rust Project?

In addition to our collaboration with the Rust Foundation’s Project Directors and the Rust Project Leadership Council, several members of our team are also active in various Rust Project Teams and Working Groups. Our staff is also as active as possible in the official Rust Project Zulip channel. 

How does the Rust Foundation solicit feedback from the Rust Project and community?

We primarily rely on the Rust Foundation’s Project Directors and the Rust Project’s Leadership Council to represent the interests of and feedback from the Rust Project. 

At the wider Rust language community level, we solicit feedback and insights from our member organizations, community organizers, grantees, and others.

Initiatives

What is the Rust Foundation’s relationship with RustConf?

Rust Foundation served as a “Diamond” level sponsor of RustConf – the Rust language community’s largest annual gathering. Our participation was limited to the typical duties of a sponsor. We were not involved in event planning, selecting talks, or managing sponsor relations.

In 2024, the Rust Foundation transitioned away from RustConf sponsorship to begin managing the event planning and hosting process. We have absorbed the financial and administrative responsibilities of running a conference. The program committee and talk selection process remains independent, with the Foundation acting only as a facilitator.

What is the Rust Foundation Security Initiative?

The Rust Foundation created its Security Initiative in 2021 to advance the state of security in the Rust programming language ecosystem. Thanks to our sponsors and collaborators, we have made a number of critical improvements to Rust’s security landscape through this program. Learn more here.

What is the Rust Foundation Community Grants Program?

The Rust Foundation’s Community Grants Program embodies our commitment to the health, innovation, and longevity of Rust. These awards provide financial support, education and travel stipends, and advocacy to Rust maintainers, contributors, Rust event organizers, and community members facing hardships. Learn more about our various grants and find more Community Grants Program-specific FAQs here.

What is the Rust Foundation Cloud Compute Program?

The Rust Foundation’s Cloud Compute Program exists to provide free compute for Rust Project maintainers around the world.

Through infrastructure credits provided by Amazon Web Services, Inc. (AWS), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud, Rust maintainers have free access to high-powered, cloud compute that dramatically reduces the time spent building and testing language changes and updates.

Learn more about the Rust Foundation Cloud Compute Program here.
