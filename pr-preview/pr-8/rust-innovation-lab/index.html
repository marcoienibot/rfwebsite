Welcome to the Rust Innovation Lab.

Guided by Rust values. Designed for your project’s success — The Rust Innovation Lab is the Rust Foundation’s home for projects that power critical infrastructure—providing trusted support, stability, and space to grow.

As the most impactful Rust projects evolve into essential infrastructure for the ecosystem, the Rust Innovation Lab (RIL) provides fiscal stewardship, governance, and administrative support for fully funded initiatives, helping them thrive while safeguarding their independence and vision.

Through the Rust Innovation Lab, funded projects gain the strength of a neutral nonprofit organization, expert guidance, and access to the wider Rust ecosystem. Together, the Rust Foundation and projects hosted through the Rust Innovation Lab can ensure that key Rust tools and efforts remain secure, sustainable, and independent for the long term.

The Rust Innovation Lab is just getting started. Stay tuned for more information, resources, case studies, and stories about the Foundation’s hosted projects.

The Rust Innovation Lab Advantage

Open source isn’t just about code—it’s about people, knowledge-sharing, and collaboration. At the Rust Foundation, we believe that when maintainers and their projects are supported, the entire ecosystem benefits. The Rust Innovation Lab was created to provide that support. Here’s what hosted projects will gain from the Rust Foundation as this program evolves…

A Stable, Neutral Home

The Rust Foundation is an independent nonprofit dedicated to stewarding the Rust Programming Language. Hosting your project with us ensures that it is supported by transparent governance and vendor-neutral backing, building confidence among contributors, partners, and users.

Governance Support

As projects grow, so do questions about leadership, decision-making, and sustainability. Innovation Lab projects receive guidance to set up structures that keep momentum strong while protecting long-term health.

Legal & Administrative Confidence

Open source shouldn’t mean facing complex legal and administrative challenges alone. We help projects navigate licensing, contributor agreements, and related issues with clarity and confidence.

Community & Visibility

Through the Rust Innovation Lab, projects gain visibility across the Rust ecosystem, increasing their ability to attract contributors, partners, and financial support. Participants also benefit from peer networking opportunities to share hard-earned lessons and best practices.

Fiscal Sponsorship

The Rust Foundation functions as the fiscal sponsor of all projects in the Rust Innovation Lab. Through this role, we can accept donations, grants, and sponsorships on your behalf under our nonprofit status. This means you don’t need to create a separate legal entity or open a bank account or handle taxes. All the while, incoming funds will be earmarked for your project.

Our team will keep clear records and provide reporting so funders know where their money is going, and you know what’s available. This allows you to build trust with sponsors, contributors, and your community while focusing on what matters to you: the code.

The Rust Foundation does not provide fiscal support to hosted projects through our core funds.

RIL Projects:

Rustls

Rustls is a memory-safe, high-performance TLS library that is by far the most popular way to build secure connections in the Rust ecosystem and is one of the top 100 packages on crates.io.

AnnouncementRustls GitHub

Symposium

Symposium is a tool that automatically installs skills, MCP servers, or other extensions based on the dependencies in your Rust crate. These extensions help agents make the best use of Rust and of the crates in your project. Symposium’s ultimate goal is to make agentic development more interoperable, reliable, and token-efficient.

Announcementsymposium.dev

Wild

Wild is a linker with the goal of being very fast for iterative development. Wild is Linux-only today, but cross-platform support is a clear priority: ports to Windows and macOS are on the roadmap. The project is licensed under MIT and Apache 2.0 (the same dual license used by the Rust project itself), so the code remains freely available to everyone. Wild is maintained by David Lattimore (lead maintainer) along with a growing group of contributors. Development is supported financially by AWS.

Announcementgithub.com/wild-linker/wild

Josh

Josh (“Just One Single History”) is a Rust-based platform for scaling Git to codebases of any size, with any number of contributors, without slowing down the pace of change. Josh is one of those projects many Rust contributors rely on every day without ever thinking about it. It quietly keeps the compiler and some of the most important tools in sync, at a scale that pushed traditional Git tooling past its limits. Its maintainers have been great partners to the Rust Project, improving Josh to meet the Project’s demanding needs. It also shows the rest of the industry what a Rust-built approach to version control at scale can look like. Bringing Josh into the Rust Innovation Lab hopefully provides this critical piece of infrastructure a stable, long-term home.

Announcementgithub.com/josh-project/josh

Curious About Joining the RIL?

Browse these questions - frequently asked by prospective RIL projects like yours - to get better acquainted before applying.

What Is the Rust Innovation Lab?

The Rust Innovation Lab (RIL) is the Rust Foundation's home for open source projects that power or advance the Rust ecosystem. It provides fiscal sponsorship plus hands-on support — letting projects stay focused on their work while the Foundation handles much of the structural overhead that otherwise falls on maintainers.

Can You Explain the Benefits of Joining In More Depth?
  1. Fiscal sponsorship — The Foundation accepts donations, grants, and sponsorships on the project's behalf under its nonprofit status. No need to form a legal entity, open a bank account, or handle taxes. Funds are earmarked for the project with clear reporting.
  2. Legal & admin support — Help navigating licensing, contributor agreements, and related challenges, without maintainers needing to go it alone.
  3. Governance guidance — Support setting up leadership and decision-making structures that keep momentum strong as a project grows.
  4. Neutral, vendor-independent home — The Foundation is an independent nonprofit not controlled by any single company. Contributors, partners, and enterprise users benefit from tapping into the resources and reputation of a neutral, trustworthy entity. 
  5. Visibility — Access to the Foundation's network across the Rust Project, the community, and Rust-focused organizations, including help attracting contributors and sponsors.

Note: The Foundation does not provide direct funding to projects from its core funds. 

Why Should Existing Projects Consider Joining the RIL?

Rust Innovation Lab projects are supported by the Rust Foundation in a variety of ways, allowing them to focus less on administrative burdens and more on the interesting work. Here's what this looks like in practice:

1. Stop being your own accountant, lawyer, and HR department

Right now, if someone wants to fund your project, you either turn them down, funnel money through a personal account, or spend months forming a legal entity. The RIL fixes this immediately. The Foundation accepts donations, grants, and sponsorships on your behalf under its existing nonprofit status. Funds are earmarked for your project (not pooled) and you get transparent reporting on what's coming in and what's available to spend. No bank account, no tax filings, no legal entity to maintain. Removes a category of work that has nothing to do with writing Rust. The RIL provides the infrastructure for projects to raise and manage their own funding more effectively, plus the operational and legal support to sustain your project long-term.

2. A neutral home that makes enterprise users comfortable

If your project is used in production by companies (or you want it to be) vendor neutrality matters more than you might expect. A project hosted by a company raises questions about roadmap influence. A project hosted by an independent nonprofit doesn't. The Foundation's governance is transparent and its independence is real. That distinction directly affects whether large organizations will commit to your project, contribute to it, or fund it.

Being part of the RIL signals long-term stability in a way that a GitHub org under a maintainer's personal account simply can't.

3. Governance support if desired

Most open source projects don't think about governance until something goes wrong (a maintainer burns out, a dispute stalls decision-making, a contributor base grows at a rapid pace). The RIL can provide guidance on setting up structures that handle situations like: how decisions get made, how leadership transitions, how sustainability is built in. 

4. Legal clarity

Licensing questions, contributor agreements, IP ownership: these come up eventually for any project with traction. Navigating them alone is slow, expensive, and stressful. RIL projects can work through these with the Foundation rather than figuring it out from scratch or paying for outside legal help out of pocket.

5. Real ecosystem connections

The Foundation has direct relationships with the Rust Project leadership, Rust-focused companies, and the broader community. For RIL projects, this means getting visibility, finding contributors, and connecting with potential sponsors. We understand the funding, governance, and organizational side and are here to support you. 

What Are the Eligibility Requirements?

The Rust Innovation Lab welcomes open source projects of all kinds that use, or focus on, the Rust Programming Language and that align with the goals and values of the Rust Foundation. Projects can be from anywhere in the world, and include software projects, open source conferences, open source research efforts, etc — as long as it's clearly beneficial for the Rust ecosystem.

Not every project will be the right fit for the Rust Innovation Lab. We review each application carefully to ensure it aligns with our mission, strategy and values and its purpose is compatible with our nonprofit status. If we feel there’s a mismatch, we may decline your application. While our decision is final, you're always welcome to reapply in the future when your project meets our requirements.

The conditions of membership include providing clear processes for the operation/cessation of the project, details of official project personnel, and any other required information to ensure legal and fiscal compliance.

Minimum Requirements

To be eligible for inclusion into the RIL, the following baseline criteria must be met:

  1. Applicant(s) must have an appropriate level of access and standing in the project's community to act as a representative (i.e., be a maintainer, not just a contributor).
  2. The project must demonstrate recent, active development.
  3. The project must be able to demonstrate a meaningful impact within the Rust ecosystem, or, for new projects, be able to articulate its potential impact.
  4. The project must be original or a substantial fork of an existing project.
  5. The project must be appropriately licensed and operate according to the principles of The Open Source Definition.
  6. The project must have an appropriate governance structure.
  7. The project must be able to sustain its own funding requirements.
  8. The project must agree to follow the Rust Foundation Code of Conduct and implement a Privacy Policy if they are handling Personally Identifiable Information (PII).

Requirement Details

To be fiscally hosted by the Rust Foundation, your open source software project must meet the following requirements:

Track record and legitimacy of the project

We assess ownership, activity, usage, and originality when making decisions about whether a project is a good fit for the Rust Innovation Lab.

  • Applicant(s) must have an appropriate level of access and standing in the project's community to act as a representative (i.e. be a maintainer, not just a contributor).
  • The project should demonstrate recent, active development.
  • The project should have a significant degree of usage, and be able to point to meaningful impact within the Rust ecosystem.
  • The project should be original or a substantial fork of an existing project.

The project should be appropriately licensed and operate according to the principles of The Open Source Definition.

Standards of behavior and maturity

Successful projects are run sustainably with good management, decision-making and participation structures. We therefore look at a project's governance structure when considering fiscal sponsorship.

Good governance structures and documentation:

  • Demonstrate to potential contributors that their contributions will be welcomed and valued
  • Provide a pathway for contributors to be promoted to maintainer or leadership positions
  • Help resolve disagreements fairly

We expect all projects to follow the Rust Foundation Code of Conduct, and to implement a Privacy Policy if they are handling Personally Identifiable Information (PII).

Funding

At present, the Rust Foundation can only accept projects that are able to sustain its own funding requirements. This means if funding is required, they have already secured financial support from external sources, or are in the process of doing so. The Rust Foundation is not able to provide funding for projects.

What Kind of Projects Are You Looking For?

The following factors make a project a compelling fit for the RIL. Not every project will excel on every dimension, but stronger candidates will demonstrate several of these qualities:

  1. Showcases Rust's strengths — The project demonstrates what Rust is capable of, serving as a compelling example of Rust's value proposition. These are important, influential, or impactful Rust projects that highlight the language's benefits.
  2. Fills a gap* for Rust developers — The project addresses a real need for people writing or using Rust. This could be tooling, libraries, infrastructure, specifications, or other resources that make Rust developers more productive, secure, or effective.
  3. RIL adds unique value — The Rust Foundation's connections to the Rust Project, Rust community, and Rust-focused organizations provide something the project couldn't easily get elsewhere. The Foundation's experience with Rust and its ecosystem is a genuine asset to the project.

* On "filling a gap":

Some gaps are obvious: "I need to debug my Rust code" is a clear need that rust-gdb addresses directly. Other gaps require more explanation. They may involve strategic concerns about Rust's future, or needs that only become apparent when you understand the broader ecosystem dynamics.

Both kinds of gaps are valid. However, the less obvious the gap, the more clearly the project should articulate why it matters to Rust developers and Rust's future. A project filling a non-obvious gap should make a compelling case for why that gap is important.
